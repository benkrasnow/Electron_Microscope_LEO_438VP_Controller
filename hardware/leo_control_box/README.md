# LEO 438VP control box (KiCad 10 project)

Microcontroller board that owns the SEM's machine state and safety, independent of the PC: EHT, stage/vacuum and VP-controller fiber links, the EO board's four fibers (register link plus the internal-scan backup), a spare I²C expansion port, isolated sync outputs to the OBI, three front-panel pushbutton inputs, an RTC backup cell, a USB-C power input, and a 2×10 IDC header for a 4.3″ 800×480 touch display (Newhaven NHD-4.3-800480FT-CSXP-CTP, FT813 EVE). (PMT signal and gain are handled by separate hardware.) Interface specs: `../../specifications/`.

Status (2026-11-14): **schematic rev 0.1 drawn, ERC clean (0 violations).** PCB not started; the board outline is still the 160 × 100 mm template.

The schematic is generated by `tools/build_schematic.py` (one A2 sheet in functional blocks: power, controller, 6 fiber transmitters, 4 fiber receivers, I²C, isolated OBI sync, display, RTC battery, pushbuttons). Connections are made with net labels and power symbols placed on the pin ends; each MOSFET gate sits directly on its buffer output. **Re-running the script overwrites the schematic**, so either keep editing the script, or switch to hand-editing in KiCad once the circuit is settled (and stop using the script).

## Teensy 4.0 vs 4.1

The 4.0 works. It has everything this board needs on its 24 edge I/O pins: 7 hardware UARTs (we use 3), FlexIO1 on pins 2–5 for the EO frames, I²C on 18/19; all 24 edge pins are now used. It lacks the 4.1's Ethernet, so the PC link is USB. **Cut the Teensy's VUSB–VIN pad before fitting it.** The board's 5 V (from USB-C J10) feeds VIN; with the pad intact that 5 V would also drive the PC's USB port, and the PC could power the board. Cut, the PC link carries data only, so plugging or resetting the PC cannot reset the controller (the Teensy does not reset when a serial port is opened). Use a 4.1 only if Ethernet isolation from the PC becomes a requirement.

Teensy 4.x pins are **3.3 V only (not 5 V tolerant)**: every 5 V signal must be level-shifted (see the receivers below).

## Circuit notes

**Fiber transmitter channel (×6).**
Teensy pin → 74AHCT125 gate (VCC = 5 V, OE tied low; its TTL input thresholds accept 3.3 V) → 2N7002 gate → HFBR-1521Z cathode. The anode goes through a current-setting resistor to +5 V (≈ 56 Ω for ~55 mA; set from the datasheet's recommended drive for 5 MBd). Add a footprint for the datasheet's peaking capacitor across the resistor.
- Logic high = LED on = line idle, which matches all links (idle high).
- **10 kΩ pull-up to 3.3 V on each AHCT input**, so the lines idle high (no false start bits) while the Teensy is in reset or being programmed.

**Fiber receiver channel (×4).**
HFBR-2521Z VCC from +5 V, with the decoupling/filter network recommended in the datasheet. VO is an open-collector output: pull it up to **3.3 V** (1 kΩ) and connect it straight to the Teensy pin, leaving RL (the internal 5 V load resistor) unconnected. That keeps the Teensy pin at a safe 3.3 V level. Check the rise time against the 271 ns EO bit period on the first board (~15 pF × 1 kΩ ≈ 15 ns, fine).

**VP controller link (fiber, replaced RS-232 on 2026-11-14).**
The VP control board (98/46) has an unused Versatile Link fiber port (TX1 HFBR-1524, RX1 HFBR-2524) wired, through its 4-way DIP switch, to the same MC68HC705C8 SCI pins as its DE-9. Set its switches 2 and 4 ON and 1 and 3 OFF (1 = brown, TX to RS-232; 2 = red, TX to fiber; 3 = orange, RX from RS-232; 4 = yellow, RX from fiber); never 3 and 4 together. Same 9600 8N1 protocol as before (`specifications/VP_CONTROLLER_INTERFACE_SPEC.md`). With this, every connection from the box to the SEM is fiber.
- Still to confirm on the VP board: idle polarity of its fiber port (is TX1 lit at idle?) and the RX1 path (that trace is on the top layer).
- Our channel uses an HFBR-1521Z / HFBR-2521Z like the others. Their 1524/2524 pair is the slower, high-sensitivity grade: with 55 mA into our transmitter their receiver may be overdriven. If the link is marginal, raise R25 (less LED current) or fit an HFBR-1524/2524 pair here (same footprints).

**I²C.**
3.3 V bus with 2.2 kΩ pull-ups on two Qwiic sockets, kept as a spare expansion port (no devices on board).

**OBI sync (isolated).**
Teensy 6/9 → ISO6720FBDR side 1 (+3V3, board GND) → side 2 → 56 Ω → J6. Side 2 is powered from the OBI end through J6 pin 1 (the Glasgow port's Vio, 1.8–5 V; the isolator draws a few mA) and its ground is SYNC_GND, which must stay separate from board GND in layout (keep the isolation gap under U13 clear of copper). This breaks the ground loop PC → USB → Teensy → sync cable → OBI → USB → PC. The F variant's outputs default low when side 1 is unpowered, so a powered-down box sends no sync edges.

**Pushbuttons.**
Three panel buttons on J11 (button to GND). Each input has a 10 kΩ pull-up and 100 nF to GND at the header (≈1 ms release RC) and a 1 kΩ series resistor to the Teensy pin. Button 1 (red) is intended as the safe-state button (EHT and filament off, following the filament-off rule); 2 and 3 are unassigned. Debounce in firmware as well.

**RTC backup.**
BT1 (CR2032 with solder tabs) keeps the Teensy's real-time clock running while the box is off, so log entries carry real timestamps. The Teensy 4.0's VBAT is a pad on its underside, so run a short wire from TP1 to that pad. The cell is only drawn on while the board is unpowered.

**Power.**
5 V from the USB-C receptacle J10 (the only supply input) → PTC fuse → +5 V rail (HFBR parts, AHCT drivers, Teensy VIN). The Teensy's 3.3 V regulator feeds the isolator, the display logic and the pull-ups (it can supply ~250 mA).

## USB-C power input (added 2026-11-14)

J10 is a power-only USB-C receptacle (GCT USB4125-GF-A, 2073-USB4125-GF-ACT-ND, $0.61). VBUS goes to +5V_IN, ahead of the PTC fuse (the terminal block J1 was removed: USB-C is the only input); the shield and GND go to ground. CC1 and CC2 each have a 5.1 kΩ pull-down (R65, R66), which identifies the board as a USB sink, so USB-C-to-C chargers switch on their 5 V output. USB-A wall adapters with an A-to-C cable supply 5 V regardless.

- No PD controller: the board only ever gets 5 V. It does not read the charger's advertised current (Default / 1.5 A / 3 A), so use an adapter rated **at least 1.5 A** (the board draws about 0.72 A with the display).
